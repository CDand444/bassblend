          Blend the subs with your speakers

          Plays a test through the receiver: first your front speakers on their own, then again with the subs. htTamer works out whether subs and speakers add up or cancel around the crossover, and how to fix the timing and level. Volume stays where you set it; everything is put back afterwards.

          Blind A/B test
          
      
          Find out which of two slots you really prefer without knowing which is which. In each round the two slots hide behind buttons 1 and 2, in a new random order. Switch between them as often as you like, then pick the one you prefer. At the end htTamer shows which slot won and how likely that is by chance, and puts the slot you were on back. It only switches slots: play your own music or film, at your usual volume. Louder usually sounds better, so compare slots at the same level.

          System report
          
      
          A full-range check of your front speakers and subs, the way you listen: a 20 Hz to 20 kHz sweep on the left speaker, the right, then both. About a minute. It finds things that are hard to hear on your own (polarity, timing, reflections, resonances, how long the room rings, distortion, noise) and says what each means and what to do.
          
      
          
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
        
      
          
      
          
      
          
      
          
    
          

    
          
      
          All speakers experimental
          
      
          Checks every bed speaker (front, center, surrounds, surround backs and the LFE) for level and distance against the front left, with a 7.1 test file that plays a sweep on each speaker in turn. Height speakers need Dolby Atmos content, so they aren’t covered.
          
      
          
        
        
        
        
        
          
      
          
      
          
      
          
    
          

    
          
      
          Sub headroom
          
      
          How loud each sub can play cleanly in your room. Tone bursts at 25, 40 and 63 Hz start quiet and rise 3 dB at a time; the test stops by itself at the first sign of strain (distortion, the sub no longer getting louder, the mic close to overload, or 105 dB at the mic). It gets loud.
