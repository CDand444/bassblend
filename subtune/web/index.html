          Live EQ

          A touch-up tool: your own EQ bands on top of the playing tune, reaching the miniDSP within a second, with a live analyzer to watch the bass at the mic. The tune itself balances every seat; use this for a last tweak at one seat or to experiment. If you use Dirac Live, bands that you keep mean running Dirac again.

          Your EQ

          Drag a numbered dot: left and right sets the frequency, up and down sets the gain. Scroll on it (or use the Q box) for width. The same bands apply to every sub.

          Saved tunes

          DSP slots

          The miniDSP holds four slots. Click one to make it play; switching back and forth is the easiest way to compare tunes by ear.

          Your tunes

          Check-up

          A 20-second health check, like the tests in pro measurement software, explained in plain words. It listens to the room for 3 seconds, then plays one sweep through all subs with your current tune.

          System report

          A full-range check of your front speakers and subs, the way you listen: a 20 Hz to 20 kHz sweep on the left speaker, the right, then both. About a minute. It finds things that are hard to hear on your own (polarity, timing, reflections, resonances, how long the room rings, distortion, noise) and says what each means and what to do.

          All speakers experimental

          Checks every bed speaker (front, centre, surrounds, surround backs and the LFE) for level and distance against the front left, with a 7.1 test file that plays a sweep on each speaker in turn. Height speakers need Dolby Atmos content, so they aren’t covered.

          Sub headroom

          How loud each sub can play cleanly in your room. Tone bursts at 25, 40 and 63 Hz start quiet and rise 3 dB at a time; the test stops by itself at the first sign of strain (distortion, the sub no longer getting louder, the mic close to overload, or 105 dB at the mic). It gets loud.

          Speaker placement

          Where each speaker of your layout should go, worked out from your room and main seat with Dolby’s home-theater guidelines. Compare with where yours are now; small differences are fine.

          Room treatment

          What acoustic treatment would help your room most, and where it goes: worked out from your room, seats and speakers, the room modes and your measurements. No sound plays here.
