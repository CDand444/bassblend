Safety and responsibility

Use at your own risk.htTamer is provided "as is", without warranty of any kind, under the PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0. The htTamer authors are not liable for damage to equipment, property or hearing, or for any other loss, however it is caused.

htTamer plays test tones, sweeps and noise through your receiver, speakers and subwoofers, changes the receiver's volume and input, and writes settings to your miniDSP. Loud sound, low bass especially, can damage speakers, subwoofers, amplifiers and hearing.

  • Start low. Begin with the receiver volume low. The automatic level setting never goes above its ceiling (70 at most) and always puts your volume back afterwards, but you are responsible for what your system can take.
  • Stay safe during loud tests. The sub headroom test plays loud bass on purpose. Keep people and pets out of the room, and press Stop at the first sign of strain: distortion, rattling, chuffing ports, or a smell of heat.
  • Check results by ear. Settings are calculated from measurements and can be wrong (a moved mic, a noisy room, a cable on the wrong input). Listen before you rely on a change.
  • Your original stays put. Tuning, tests and the assistant never write miniDSP slot 1, so your own setup is always one switch away. Only you can overwrite it, on the Saved tunes page, after a warning you have to confirm.
  • Not affiliated. htTamer isn't made, endorsed or supported by Onkyo, Denon, Marantz, JBL, miniDSP, Dirac or any other company whose products it works with.

The first time htTamer opens on a device it shows these terms and won't change anything until they're accepted. Pages that play sound or write the miniDSP carry a reminder at the top.

What htTamer does

htTamer tunes the subwoofers of a home theater. The receiver's subwoofer output goes into a miniDSP 2x4HD, which feeds each sub. htTamer measures every sub with a UMIK-1 mic at your seats and works out a gain, a delay, a polarity and EQ filters for each sub. It then writes them into one of the miniDSP's four slots.

It also checks how the subs blend with your speakers through the receiver, runs a full-range system report, gives you a live EQ with a live analyzer, and has an assistant that can explain results and run tests for you.

The chain is: source → receiver (Dirac Live, bass management) → sub out → miniDSP → subs. htTamer controls the miniDSP and some receiver settings (input, volume during tests, sub level, listening mode). It can't change Dirac Live or the receiver's speaker setup menu, so it tells you exactly what to change there.

First-time setup

Work down Setup → Checklist. Each line turns green when it's done.

  1. Mic calibration: upload your UMIK-1's calibration file (the one for its serial number). Every measurement uses it.
  2. miniDSP: connected over USB to the Pi. htTamer owns one slot (slot 3 here). Slot 1 keeps your original setup: htTamer never writes it unless you choose to overwrite it on the Saved tunes page and confirm.
  3. Receiver: its network address, so htTamer can switch inputs, set the test volume and read the sub level. The Receiver page also shows what the receiver reports about itself: whether it has a sub, each speaker's size (Small/Large), where the height speakers are, the crossover, the sub and center levels, the signal playing now and the model/firmware. htTamer checks that against your records (for example, the layout or a speaker set to Large) and can copy the sizes onto the Speakers page. The receiver can't report speaker distances, per-speaker levels or per-speaker crossovers over the network. Copy them from its Speaker Setup menu onto the Speakers page, and htTamer compares the distances with where the speakers actually are.
  4. Room: the room size (and its shape if it isn't a rectangle), your seats, couches and soffits, with the plan and the 3D view.
  5. Subs: each sub's model and knob settings (ports, EQ mode, Q) and the AI's suggested setup. Some sub modes forbid EQ boost below a frequency. htTamer obeys that everywhere. Both pages save to the same place: Save room or Save subs stores everything you typed on either.
  6. Speakers: the model and position of each speaker. This doesn't change tuning, but the assistant uses it for advice.
  7. Pi HDMI (recommended): a cable from the Pi's micro-HDMI port next to the USB-C power into a free receiver HDMI input. Pick that input once on any test page. Receiver tests then play from the Pi and htTamer switches the input for you.

Rooms that aren't boxes

In Room, the Shape buttons describe a room that isn't a plain rectangle. Enter the overall length and width first, then pick L-shape, Alcove (a bump-out or bay on the back wall) or Cut corner (an angled wall), and adjust it. Custom starts from the rectangle so you can draw any outline. Everything is in feet from the left wall and the front (screen) wall, like the seats.

  • Corners: drag them on the plan (they snap to 3″ and line up with the corners next to them, which keeps walls square), or type them in the Corners list. Click + on a wall to add a corner, and ✕ in the list to remove one. The plan shows each wall's length. Length and width then come from the outline; changing them stretches it.
  • Openings, doors and windows (Add door and Add double door start from the usual 3 ft and 5 ft widths; a double door is drawn with two leaves): pick the wall (numbered like the corners: wall 1 runs from corner 1 to corner 2), how far along it from its first corner, the width, and the height (empty means floor to ceiling). An opening to another room or an open-plan space has no wall: no reflection there, no panel and no corner trap. A door counts as a wall when shut. A window reflects sound like a wall, but panels can't go on it, so the treatment plan suggests curtains or a freestanding panel there.
  • Ceiling: Sloped (give the low side and its height) or Vaulted (peaked; give the height at the sides and which way the peak runs). The room's Height is always the highest point.

What uses the real shape: the first-reflection points and treatment zones land on your actual walls (a wall in the way, such as an L's inside corner, blocks a reflection), the bass-trap corners are the room's real outside corners, the back wall is the one straight behind the seats, and the suggested Dolby speaker spots are on real walls inside the room. Room modes are only exact for a box. For any other shape htTamer calculates them for the room's main box (the biggest rectangle inside it), marks them estimate, and lists rough extra modes from the rest of the room (dashed, with ~). Openings and sloped ceilings get a note too. Your measurements (check-up, system report) show the real modes.

Build it with your phone

Floor plan on this computer? Press Upload floor plan above the room plan, pick the image (for a PDF, a screenshot of the page), say which room it is if the plan shows more, and press Read the plan. The AI's reading fills in the shape, openings and sizes in the editor here, not saved: check it in the plan or 3D view, adjust, place the seats and press Save, or press Undo. Sizes you've typed win over the plan; one AI request.

Press Build with your phone above the room plan and scan the QR code (or, to upload a floor plan file that's on your computer, use Open the room builder on this computer under the code) with your phone's camera (the phone must be on the same Wi-Fi as htTamer). The code signs that phone in once and works for 10 minutes. On the phone:

  1. Type the overall length, width and height (feet, the height at its highest) and notes on anything that isn't a box: an L, an alcove, an opening to the kitchen, a sloping ceiling.
  2. Have a floor plan? Press Upload floor plan: an architect's or estate agent's plan, a sketch with sizes, or a screenshot from a floor plan app (for a PDF, take a screenshot of the page). The AI takes the shape, the doors and openings and the sizes written on it, turns the plan so the screen wall is the front, and says so. If the plan shows the whole house, say in the notes which room it is. Your typed sizes still win.
  3. Add photos (up to 8, together with the plan): stand in a corner and photograph the opposite wall, corner by corner, plus the openings and the ceiling if it slopes. Say which wall each photo shows; the screen wall is the front.
  4. Press Build my room with AI. Your AI assistant reads the photos, sizes and notes and draws the room: turn it around in 3D (drag, pinch to zoom) or see the 2D plan with wall numbers. It lists what it assumed and what you should check.
  5. If something is off, tell it ("the alcove is 4 ft deep") and press Rebuild. When it looks right, press Save to htTamer. Nothing is saved before that.

The room builder needs the AI assistant (an API key under Tools → AI analysis; Anthropic, OpenAI and Gemini all read photos), and each build counts as one request against the assistant's daily limit. The sizes you type always win over what the AI estimates from photos. iPhones can add the page to the home screen (Share → Add to Home Screen); if it then asks for your PIN, that's normal, since home-screen apps keep their own sign-in. A web page can't use the iPhone's LiDAR scanner (only native apps can), so the builder works from photos, sizes and notes. Fine-tune the result on the dashboard: drag the corners, adjust openings, and place the seats. The 3D button above the plan shows the same room in 3D there too.

The assistant can record the shape too: describe the room ("L-shaped, 20 by 23 feet, the back right 7 by 10 feet is the kitchen, open") and it saves the outline and openings. Rooms saved before shapes existed are rectangles and keep working as before.

Pick screen wall does it in one step: press it, then click the wall the screen is really on in the plan, and the room turns so that wall becomes the front wall (it works for any square-on wall; you can undo it). The Turn left, Turn right, Turn 180 and Mirror buttons above the plan turn the whole room — outline, doors, windows, the ceiling slope, and every seat, sub and speaker — so you can line the screen up with the right wall without re-entering anything. They save the room, and Undo turn steps back up to 10 turns.

Tuning the subs

Tune → Tune my system walks you through it.

  1. Auto-tune measures each sub on its own at several mic spots. The first spot is your main listening position (MLP); move the mic when asked. Each spot is a short sweep per sub.
  2. htTamer then works out the best gain, delay, polarity and EQ so all seats are as even and as close to your goal curve as possible. The goal is flat, plus the "house curve" bass lift set in the target settings.
  3. The result is applied to htTamer's slot and saved in Tunes & slots. Every tune you ever applied is there, so you can always go back.
  4. Verify plays one sweep with all subs on and compares the real result with what was expected. Within about 1 dB means the tune works as planned.

The Result chart shows "how it was" (measured), "expected after" (dashed) and "actual after" (verified). The shaded band is the range being tuned.

When the result isn't great. Under the result, htTamer says what limits the tune when it is only fair or poor, and what would help most, with a link to the right page. EQ flattens peaks, but it can't fill a cancellation (a deep, narrow dip caused by the room), can't make seats in different spots sound alike, and can't shorten the room's ringing. So the advice is often physical: try a sub in another place, move the main seat a little, add bass traps (from about 40 Hz up; below that only placement and EQ help), give the tune more EQ bands, or trim what's left with Live EQ. The edges of the tuned range are where the subs roll off, so they aren't counted as faults. The assistant sees the same assessment.

An infrasonic sub

Have one sub that goes much deeper than the others (a big sealed sub, or a ported one tuned low)? Set its Role to Infrasonic on Room & subs. That sub keeps playing everything, and the lowest notes go to it alone: the other subs get a 24 dB per octave high-pass at the Infrasonic cutoff (Tune → target settings, 20 Hz by default, 12 to 40 Hz). That keeps small or ported subs out of the range they can't play cleanly and lets them play louder above it. Only one sub can be the infrasonic one.

Optimize (or auto-tune) again afterwards: the fit then chooses gain, delay and EQ for the system as it will really play, with the other subs filtered. The "how it was" curve stays as measured. The high-pass is written to the other subs' outputs on htTamer's slot with the tune (never slot 1), the tune table marks them high-pass 20 Hz, and a tune keeps its own cutoff, so changing the setting later only affects new tunes. To get the most from it, lower the Lowest frequency in the target settings (for example to 15 Hz) so the fit looks below the cutoff.

Test tones follow the split. The infrasonic sub's Play test tone offers deep tones (down to 12 Hz, but never below a ported sub's safe limit), and the other subs' tones start at the cutoff, where their high-pass begins. Each sub's card shows its range; tones outside it are refused, from the page and from the assistant alike. Without an infrasonic sub, tones stay 20 to 200 Hz.

Bass shakers

Have tactile transducers (bass shakers) on their own amplifier? On Room & subs, press Use for a bass shaker on a free output's card. A shaker is felt at the seat, not heard: it gets the subs' bass, band-limited by a high- and low-pass around the range it can move, at its own level, and timed to what the main seat hears. The Bass shakers block below the sub cards sets the level (dB), the high- and low-pass (Hz), the delay and the night setting, then Save shaker settings. Adding, removing or changing anything writes nothing to the miniDSP until you press Apply the tune again: one rewrite of htTamer's slot with the playing tune and the shaker settings, no new measuring. The shaker's card (and the block) say when the playing tune doesn't feed it yet.

Timing. Delay Auto works it out from the tuned subs' delays and the distance from the subs to the seat (shown as "auto: X ms"); Custom adds your own 0-30 ms on top of the subs' delays. Test tones are felt, not heard: the shaker card's Play test tone offers the frequencies inside the shakers' band and its result says "Played … feel it at the seat" — the mic barely hears a shaker, so that is the good result. Night mode with Keep the shakers on checked mutes only the subs, so the shakers keep going (on htTamer's slot only; slot 1 is never touched).

When to adjust the timing. A shaker doesn't add to the subs' sound, so there is no cancellation to fix: its "phase" is only timing, the punch you feel against the bass you hear, and Auto is right for most rooms. If you use Custom, judge it with music with a sharp kick drum or a movie hit, not the test tone: a steady tone can't show timing. If the punch arrives before the sound, add delay, 2 ms at a time; if it feels late, soft or doubled, take some off. Differences under about 3 ms can't be felt, so stop when it feels like one hit, and re-check after moving the subs or the seat, or after a new tune (Auto follows by itself). Boomy or slow-feeling shaking isn't timing — lower the low-pass or the level instead. Polarity doesn't matter for a shaker, so there is no invert.

Removing a sub

Unplugged a sub, sold it or moved it to another room? On Room & subs, press Remove this sub on its output. htTamer stops using that output for a sub: auto-tune, the optimizer and the tests leave it out. You choose whether to keep its details (model, position, knobs) for when it comes back, or forget them. Removing it writes nothing to the miniDSP.

Tunes made with that sub are marked stale in Tunes & slots and can't be applied, because they would still drive it. Optimize again: each sub was measured on its own, so the other subs' measurements still count and no new sweeps are needed (re-measuring is better if you moved anything). Until a new tune is applied, the playing one still sends bass to that output. If that matters (a sub that is still plugged in, or an amplifier on that output), the sub's card offers Mute it on the miniDSP: one write to htTamer's slot, only while that slot is the one playing, never slot 1. Any new tune applied there keeps the output muted.

Changed your mind? Use for a sub on the output's card puts it back; measure it before it gets a tune. The assistant can do all three too, after you approve.

A sub added after the playing tune needs a tune. On htTamer's slot the miniDSP only sends the input to the outputs the playing tune includes, so a sub you add later stays silent until you tune again (Tune my system). Until then its card says so with a Tune my system button, Home → Is it ready? flags it, and its test tone is refused with that reason instead of playing silence.

Blending subs and speakers

Even a perfect sub tune can fight the speakers around the crossover (typically 60-120 Hz). The Blend test plays sweeps through the receiver: the front speaker alone, then the subs together with it. It reports how much bass is lost where they cancel ("dB lost"); under 1 dB is great.

  • Measure at the MLP, then at 2-3 more seats. htTamer finds one setting that works for all measured seats, weighting the MLP more.
  • Apply to all seats bakes the fix (sub delay, polarity flip, optionally a phase filter) into a new tune on the playing slot. Measure the seats again afterwards: old seats belong to the old tune.
  • If the subs arrive late, only the receiver can fix it: increase its Subwoofer distance by the amount shown.
  • Sub level: the button sets an exact level worked out from the measurement ("measured at −11 dB, 2.7 dB too quiet → set −8.5 dB"). It doesn't add to whatever the receiver is set to now, so pressing it again or refreshing the page doesn't move the target. Once the receiver is there, it shows ✓.

Live EQ

Live EQ lets you add your own EQ bands on top of the playing tune and hear (and see) the change straight away.

How it works

  • Each sub output on the miniDSP has 10 EQ bands. A tune uses some of them (for example 5). Live EQ puts your bands in the free ones at the top, up to 4, and uses the same bands on every sub.
  • Only those bands are written, so a change reaches the miniDSP in under a second and the tune's own filters, gains, delays and polarity are never touched. Your bands only go to htTamer's slot, and only while it's the one playing.
  • Each band is a peaking filter: a frequency (where), a gain (how much up or down) and a Q (how narrow: Q 5-8 is a narrow notch for one room resonance, Q 1-2 a broad hump).
  • Bypass switches your bands off and on so you can compare (A/B). Turn off takes them off the miniDSP but keeps them on the page. Applying any tune also switches them off, because it rewrites every band.
  • Save into a new tune copies the playing tune, adds your bands to every sub's filters and applies the copy. The old tune stays in Tunes & slots.

Using the editor

  • Add band creates a band. When the analyzer is running it starts on the biggest peak above your goal.
  • Drag a numbered dot: left and right moves the frequency, up and down sets the gain. Scroll on a dot to change its width, or type exact numbers in the table.
  • The shaded red area at the left is where boosting is not allowed (ported subs in their current mode). The page also warns if your bands push a sub output above 0 dB inside the miniDSP, which can clip on loud scenes.

The live analyzer

  • Start analyzer sets the volume with the guarded auto-level, then plays soft bass noise (15-200 Hz) through the receiver in Stereo, so bass management sends it to the subs. It plays from the Pi's HDMI or the network player.
  • The orange line is the live response at the mic (calibrated, 1/6-octave smoothed, about 1 second of averaging). The dotted line is your goal. Both are shown around their average, so you're looking at shape, not loudness.
  • Hold curve keeps a copy so you can compare before and after a change. Response chooses how quickly the curve follows (Fast is jumpier, Slow is steadier).
  • It stops by itself after 10 minutes, when you leave the page, or if the mic hears it far louder than your test level. The receiver input, mode and volume are put back when it stops.

Auto-fit

With the analyzer running, Auto-fit works out the bands for you in about a second: it finds the peaks above your goal inside the tuned range and fits cut-only bands (frequency, depth and width) that bring the curve closest to the goal. It uses only as many free bands as actually help. They're sent straight away, and the curve from before is kept as the held curve so you can compare. Press it again after a few seconds to refine: it takes the bands that were playing out of the measurement first, so it improves them instead of stacking more. It gives the same answer for the same room every time and costs nothing to run.

It only cuts. A narrow dip is usually a cancellation that boosting can't fill and that only wastes amplifier power: fix those with placement, the blend test or the seat.

Asking the assistant

Ask the assistant to do it hands the same job to the chat. It starts the analyzer (you approve the sound once), runs the auto-fit, checks that the live curve did what the fit expected, refines if not, and explains the result in plain words, including anything EQ can't fix. You can also ask it for specific changes ("make the 42 Hz cut narrower"). It won't save the bands into a tune unless you ask (and approve).

Tips

  • Keep the mic at the seat you're EQ'ing (normally the MLP). Live EQ helps that seat most; the tune itself balances all seats.
  • Cut peaks, don't fill dips. A peak that moves a lot when you move the mic a little is a seat-specific room mode: be gentle with it.
  • If the analyzer says the noise is only a few dB above the room, raise the volume a little.

Calibration report

Home → Calibration report opens one printable page with everything about the current calibration: the system, the playing tune (with its predicted, goal and measured curves), the blend across seats, gain structure and protective limiters, the receiver's speaker settings, what the fixes changed, open findings and next steps. Every figure is dated with the measurement it came from. Use Print / save as PDF to keep a copy.

Protecting the subs

  • Protective limiters (Measure → Sub headroom): after the headroom test, htTamer can set a limiter on each sub output just below where that sub started to strain. Normal listening never reaches it; it only stops a loud scene or a volume slip from pushing a sub into damage. It stays with every tune you apply.
  • Gain structure (Setup → Checklist): checks that the receiver's sub output can't overload the miniDSP's input at the loudest you listen, with full-scale LFE. Clipping there happens before any tune, so EQ can't fix it. It plays a quiet 40 Hz tone for a few seconds.

System report and fixes

Measure → System report plays full-range sweeps on the left, right and both front speakers with the subs. It reports the response against your goal per range, peaks and dips and their likely cause (room resonance, wall cancellation, reflection), left/right balance and timing, reverberation, distortion, noise, and bass extension. Each finding says who can fix it: htTamer, Dirac, the receiver menu (with the exact setting), or your hands (placement, furnishings, wiring).

Fix what htTamer can does the automatic part: sets the receiver's sub level, runs a blend test and applies the blend fix, then runs the report again.

What htTamer changed

Home shows one log of every fix since a baseline (the tune and receiver sub level before the first fix) and the net result, for example: "Subs +1.75 ms later, polarity flipped compared with 'Single MLP'; receiver sub level −15 → −11.5 dB". htTamer stops stacking blend fixes when they stop settling: more than two, tests that contradict each other, or a sub level that bounces up and down.

  • Keep these changes: what's playing becomes the new baseline.
  • Undo all fixes: the baseline tune and sub level go back.

Other tests

  • All speakers (needs the Pi's HDMI): a sweep on every bed speaker and the LFE from a 7.1 file. It checks each speaker's level and arrival against the front left. Height speakers can't be tested this way.
  • Check-up: a 20-second health check at a spot: room noise, distortion, bass ringing and measurement quality.
  • Measurements: each sub's raw response per spot, the data tunes are built from.
  • Level match: a short rumble on each sub in turn to compare their loudness.
  • Sub headroom: loud. Rising tone bursts on one sub until it distorts or compresses, to see how loud it plays cleanly.
  • Level meter: a calibrated sound level meter (C-weighted and bass-only), accurate to about ±1-2 dB.

Soffits: a soffit (bulkhead or drop ceiling) is a box along a wall that lowers the ceiling there, common around the screen or over ducts. Under Soffits in the shape block, add one, pick its wall, then set where it starts along the wall, how long it is, how far it sticks out and how high its underside is. A soffit longer than its wall is trimmed to the wall, and its ends are cut by the walls beside it, so next to an angled wall it blends into that wall. The plan draws it hatched and the 3D view as a box under the ceiling. Height speaker spots under it sit at its underside, and when a soffit runs along the front wall the treatment plan adds a note about in-ceiling and height speakers close to that wall. Soffits are small, so the bass (room mode) numbers ignore them. Turning the room turns the soffits with it. Pointing at or clicking into a soffit, opening or corner row in the shape block lights it up on the plan (the wall it is on gets a bar, the soffit or opening itself is filled in), so you can see where a number belongs.

AI suggestions are folded. Everything the AI writes for you (the suggested setup, the per-sub suggestions, the room analysis, and the phone builder's assumptions and questions) sits in a closed fold marked AI: open it when you want to read it, and it stays as you left it. The room analysis says when it is older than your current tune. The cards on Home that repeat the Results page (What the tune did, Result) fold from their headers too.

On Windows: HDMI, the mic and the miniDSP

The Windows app measures with the UMIK-1 and writes the miniDSP over USB with no driver. For tests that play through the receiver it can use this computer's own HDMI output: plug the PC's HDMI cable into a receiver input and pick that input on the test page (This computer plugged into). htTamer finds the HDMI audio output by itself; the Audio output list lets you choose another one. For the 7.1 tests (the all-speakers check) set that output to 7.1 surround in Windows (Sound settings, the output, Configure speakers): the page says so when it is set to fewer channels. If the receiver shows the surround and surround-back speakers swapped, tick Swap surround and surround back. Without HDMI, the receiver's network player (stereo) or a USB stick still work. If the receiver can't fetch a test file over the network, Windows Firewall may be blocking it: the help on that page has a command to copy and run once as administrator.

Basic and Advanced

The Basic / Advanced switch at the top changes how much you see. Basic shows only Home, Setup, Calibrate, Results and Compare (the blind A/B test), and hides the acoustic analysis pages and the header's extras until you ask for them: press Show all pages or switch to Advanced. Every page is still one click away from a link (for example "Open" on the readiness panel), and the page you are on stays in the menu. A new system starts in Basic; a system that has already been calibrated keeps the full Advanced view. Each device remembers its own choice.

Your first calibration (on Home until a tune exists) needs only the mic calibration file and your sub outputs: put the mic at the main seat and press Calibrate my subs. It measures at one seat, applies the tune and verifies it; your speakers, furniture, soffits and treatment are optional and can be added later to sharpen the advice.

Results and readiness

Verify → Results puts the answer to "did it help?" on one page: the bass at the main seat before and after (measured when you have verified the tune, otherwise labelled as what the tune expects), how alike the seats sound, how well the subs blend with the speakers at the crossover, and whether you compared by ear. It lists what improved, what did not improve and what has not been measured yet: nothing is hidden when it did not improve. A blend result only counts when it was measured for the tune that is playing now.

Home → Is it ready? shows what is connected (from the silent hardware check, with how long ago, and as Unknown when that check is old), what is set up, what has been verified on this system and when, and what has never been run. The verdict is Ready, Ready, with warnings or Not ready (a missing mic calibration file, no sub outputs, or a miniDSP that does not answer). Run the silent check plays nothing.

Rooms that aren't enclosed

A living room, or a room with a large opening, isn't a closed box, and that changes what htTamer can predict. In Room, the shape block has an Enclosure setting: Closed (walls all around), A large opening (the walls mostly stand), or Open plan (a whole side is open to another space). For the last two, Whole side open ticks the sides that have no wall, and Openings below sets the width of a smaller opening. It uses the same openings as a doorway or arch, so the plan, the 3D view and the treatment plan all follow.

  • Room modes are then a rough guide: bass escapes into the open space, so the room reinforces it less (your subs may need more output than in a closed room) and the real peaks and dips are usually smaller and in different places. The measurements are the answer.
  • Treatment: there is no wall to treat on an open side, bass traps help less, and the plan says to put absorption where walls exist (the side walls' first reflections, behind the screen).
  • The assistant and the phone room builder know about it too: tell them the room opens into the kitchen and they record it as an opening.

Where you live (house, apartment, condo)

On Setup (also in the Basic view) a quick Where is the theater? question takes the home type and whether someone lives below, above or through a wall; it saves by itself. In Room, under Where you live, say whether this is a house, townhouse, apartment or condo, which floor, whether someone lives below, above or through a wall, and whether the building is wood frame or concrete. Deep bass (below about 40 Hz) goes through floors and walls far better than anything else, so it is what neighbors notice. With a shared building htTamer:

  • offers a gentler bass lift as a step on Home (2 dB with a shared floor or ceiling, 3 dB with shared walls); it only changes the target when you apply it, and you can always set your own;
  • suggests quiet hours: in Tools → Automation, set Quiet hours start and night mode switches itself on at that time (and off at the Switch off by itself time). If you turn it off yourself in between, it stays off until the next night;
  • reminds you to run tests at a considerate hour, and adds notes to the treatment plan: freestanding panels instead of drilling, and that panels cannot stop bass leaving the room;
  • notes what the structure does to room modes (concrete rings longer, wood leaks more).

It never plays anything or changes a setting for this. A house with no shared walls gets no extra advice.

Screen

In Room, under Screen, describe what is on the front wall: a TV or a projector screen. Give its diagonal size in inches and its shape (16:9, 2.35:1 and so on); htTamer works out the width and height. Then set where its lower edge is above the floor, how far its face stands in front of the wall, and where its middle is along the wall (leave it blank to center it). The plan and the 3D view draw it to scale.

  • Acoustically transparent (AT): tick this for a perforated or woven projector screen that lets sound through, and then Front left, center and right are behind it if your speakers stand behind it. A TV can never be AT.
  • What it changes: the treatment plan notes how much of your view the screen fills from the main seat (about 30–40° is the usual target for movies), what an AT screen does to the speakers behind it (it takes a little off the highest frequencies, so run room correction with the screen in place), that a solid screen or a TV needs the center speaker above or below it, and that a TV's glass face reflects sound.
  • Choosing the front wall with Pick screen wall or the turn buttons assumes the screen is on the wall you make the front wall; Mirror flips where along it the screen's middle is.

The 3D view

The 3D button on the Room and Treatment pages shows your room as a model: walls, openings, soffits, the screen, couches and risers, speakers (cabinets with a cone, turned to face your main seat; ceiling speakers hang from the ceiling; paler ones are suggested spots), subs (numbered, in their output color) and your seats. On Treatment every item of the plan is drawn where it goes: wall panels as tiles, ceiling clouds, the rug, and the corner bass traps, in the plan's colors (green = installed). Panels on a wall that faces you fade so the room stays visible. Nothing is drawn through anything else: a trap stops short of a door, and a tile that would hit a speaker, the couch or another treatment is left out. The What to show panel beside it lists every item by value (Do first, Next, Nice to have, Already installed) with a check box for each item and for each group, plus All and None: switch things off to see how the room looks and what the rest still does. The drawing, the 3D view, the cost and a buy list (identical panels bought together, with a Copy as text button) follow your choice, which stays on this device; the plan itself does not change. A sloped-ceiling cloud follows the slope, a room that opens behind the seats gets a freestanding panel or curtain on stands, and doors get their own item (a panel or heavy curtain on each leaf of a double door, and on a single door where a reflection lands), windows in a reflection zone get a heavy curtain, and the underside of a soffit gets a thin panel. Drag to turn, scroll or pinch to zoom, double-click to reset.

Sound field

Survey → Sound field shows how bass fills your room, seen from above at a height you choose. Drag the frequency slider: the loud spots (red) and quiet spots (blue) move as the room's standing waves change, and the line under the map shows what the same model says at your seat (with your measured response over it once you have measured). Click a room mode to jump to its frequency, switch to Moving wave to watch the pressure rise and fall, and change the room's damping to see how much treatment would calm it. Your subs are the squares and your seat is the black dot. It is the standard standing-wave model for the room's box (the main rectangle of an irregular room), with the subs treated as identical and in phase, so use it to understand and to choose where things go, and measure to decide.

Beside the views from above, from the side and from the end there is a 3D view with three slices through the room that you can turn and zoom. Bass and Treble are separate layers with their own frequency sliders. Treble follows one speaker's sound to your seat and round the room: the blue line is the direct sound, orange lines are its reflections (fainter after each bounce, and gone quickly off a panel that absorbs at that frequency), small dots travel along them in slow motion, and in the views from above rings spread from the speaker and its mirror images. Slide the frequency to see walls and panels change: thin panels do little below a few hundred hertz and a lot above. The table shows the strongest early reflections at your seat. Show what goes through the walls draws arrows out of the walls: more sound goes through at low frequencies and through light walls. The treatment shown is what you have switched on in the Treatment picker, and a speaker's Faces direction matters because speakers beam more as the frequency rises.

Sub workshop

Tune → Sub workshop guides you through hands-on work with one sub. Pick the sub, then either:

  • Tune the sub's own controls (a sub picked from the catalog on the Subs page, for example a Hsu VTF-3): htTamer goes one control at a time (ports, then the EQ switch, then Q). It tells you what to set, you press Measure (one 20-second sweep through only that sub, with the mic at your seat), and it scores the result against the target curve, says in plain words what changed (more or less bass in each band, a peak, how low it reaches) and whether it beat the last one. When every choice of a control is measured the best one is kept and the next control starts from it. The last step verifies the winners together. Record these on the Subs card writes the winning settings down; set the sub itself by hand.
  • Sub crawl: put the sub at your listening seat and carry the mic to the places a sub could go (eight default spots, or your own with positions in feet). The response at each spot is what your seat would hear from a sub there, so the smoothest spots are where the sub should live. Then move the sub there, put the mic back at the seat and measure once more: htTamer compares it with what the crawl predicted. The best spot can be set as the sub's position.

Scores are lower-is-better (grades: great, good, fair, poor) and compare shape only: the overall level is set aside, so use Level match for volume. Measure again on any trial repeats it: a repeat within about 1 dB means the measurement can be trusted; a bigger difference means something moved. If a tune is playing, it is part of every measurement: the comparisons are fair, but the scores are the sub plus your tune. Nothing plays unless you press Measure and confirm, and night mode blocks it. The assistant can read a session and explain it.

Floor, and modeling risers and couches

On Room, pick what the Floor is made of (and tick Area rug if there is one): it changes the ring-time estimate, the treatment plan (a carpeted floor or a rug means no rug is suggested), the sound field and the 3D floor. Under Furniture, Model furniture with measurements or words builds a stepped riser with a couch on each row: enter rows, width, tier depth and heights and press Build the tiers (no AI), or describe it in words and press Build from the description (one AI request). The result appears in the list, plan and 3D view but is not saved until you press Save room; Undo puts the list back.

The Room page is a set of tabs (Size and floor, Shape, Where you live, Screen, Seats, Furniture, Fixtures) beside a plan or 3D view that stays in sight while you change things. Edit layout opens a top-down editor: drag couches, risers, subs and seat positions into place, add new ones from the left (click or drag), turn with R, undo with Ctrl+Z, and press Apply; Save room keeps it. Speakers, fixtures and the screen move in the editor too: wall speakers and wall lights stay on the nearest wall, the screen slides along the front wall, and Apply saves speaker positions. The editor has a Top / 3D switch: in 3D, drag an item across the floor, or drag empty space to turn the view. Risers can take the shape of the room: they follow angled walls and notches, are cut away for a door's swing, and wrap around subs, floor speakers and couches in the way. A riser's Top surface (wood, carpeted, painted, concrete) is counted in the room's ring-time estimate.

Place rows under my seats (on by default) puts each tier under a row of your seats at the height their head positions imply, and keeps it inside an angled or notched wall. With Wrap around walls and obstacles (on by default) a tier is cut to your room outline and around doors, subs, floor speakers and furniture already placed, as a few touching boxes at one height. Before the AI assistant changes anything htTamer keeps, it makes a backup (see Tools, Backups).

On Room, each sub has a Model drop-down fed by the hardware database (SVS, Hsu and more); picking one fills in the driver, enclosure, cabinet size, rated low end and amp power, and Other lets you type any model. The Speakers page does the same for speakers.

On Speakers, Aimed sets where a speaker points: Auto (in-ceiling straight down, atmos modules up to bounce off the ceiling to your seat, others at the seat), at the main seat, straight down, up with a bounce, or Custom with a compass Faces and a Tilt. The 3D views show a beam for it.

Bass analysis

Live music check. At the top of Verify → Bass analysis, choose a WAV of a song you know well (10 seconds to 3 minutes is used) and press Start the live check. htTamer plays it through the receiver and records the mic at the same time. Because it knows exactly what it sent, it shows the response, the phase and the coherence: 100% means everything the mic heard came from the music; under 50% means room noise or late echoes dominate, so don't fix what the curve shows there. It plays only when you press Start, can set the test volume (guarded, put back afterwards), and puts the receiver back as every test does.

Verify → Bass analysis reads the sweeps htTamer has already taken; nothing plays. The waterfall shows each bass frequency over the first 300 ms after the sound stops (a sweep records no more ringing than that): a red or yellow streak running far down is a room mode that keeps ringing (boomy, slow bass). Below it are the ring times (how long each part of the bass takes to fall 20 dB); around 300 ms or more is worth taming with a cut in the tune, moving a sub or seat, or a bass trap. "At least" before a time means the bass was still ringing when the recording ended, and below about 40 Hz no ring time is given: a sweep this short can't tell the room from the analysis there. Choose the mic spot and whether to see all subs with the playing tune, as measured, one sub alone, or the whole system (speakers and subs through the receiver, from the System report, whose Waterfall button opens this page). Subs and speakers: in phase? uses the blend test's measurements: where both play (the shaded band), a phase near 0° adds up and near ±180° cancels; switch between as measured and the blend test's best delay and polarity. Can this measurement be trusted? gives one answer from how far the sweep stood above the room's noise and where two takes of the same sweep disagreed (takes are recorded for measurements made from this version on): treat the named frequencies with care, or measure that spot again.

Fixtures: lights, projector, vents

In Room, under Fixtures, add what hangs from the ceiling or sits on a wall: recessed and ceiling lights, a pendant, a ceiling fan, a projector, vents and registers, sprinkler heads, wall lights. Give each its position (feet from the left wall and the front wall; for a wall light, the point on the wall and the height of its middle) and size (inches). The treatment plan then keeps clouds and panels clear: a ceiling cloud is moved off lights and the projector's beam to the screen, 18 in is kept round a sprinkler head (check your local code) and about a foot round a vent, and wall panels go around a wall light. Speakers in the walls and ceiling count already: set their kind (in-wall, on-wall, in-ceiling) on the Speakers page, and panels leave about 5 in round an on-wall or in-wall speaker. The plan notes say how to work around each (dark fabric near a projector, clouds between recessed lights, and so on), and the plans and the 3D view show them.

Sub aim, cabinet sizes and the screen frame

Sub aim. In Room, each sub has Points at (the main seat, the screen, the back, left or right wall, or a custom angle). The angle counts clockwise on the plan: 0 = at the back wall, 90 = the left wall, 180 = the screen, 270 = the right wall. The plan draws an arrow, the 3D view turns the sub, and the card says which wall it points at and how far away it is. Low bass is hardly directional, so aim matters less than position, but a driver or port a few inches from a wall is blocked and boomy; the treatment plan says so.

Cabinet sizes. Enter a sub's or speaker's width, height and depth in inches (spec sheets list them). Left blank, htTamer uses the size in the hardware database for that model, or else a typical size for that kind of cabinet, and says which. On the Speakers page, the Model is a drop-down of the speakers in the hardware database (picking one fills in its kind and size) with Other to type your own. Each speaker shows its angle to your main seat (left or right of straight ahead, above or below ear height, distance) and whether that is inside Dolby's range, and a Faces field for which way it points (blank = at the seat). Press Remember size next to a model's size (on its Subs card or its Speakers row) to put it in the hardware database, so every room with that model uses it; you can also ask the assistant to look a model up. Quiet hours can be set by the assistant too. The 3D views draw each cabinet to size.

Screen frame. Under Screen, set the frame or bezel width (a TV is about half an inch, a fixed-frame screen about three). The screen's whole size, frame included, is what the plan, the 3D view and the treatment plan use: front-wall panels stay off it unless the screen is acoustically transparent. Ceiling clouds stay clear of soffits too.

Furniture: couches and risers

In Room, under Furniture, add the couches or sofas in the room: pick a kind (loveseat, 3-seat, sectional, recliner or custom), then set its size, seat and back height, fabric, which way its back faces and where its middle is (feet from the left wall and from the front). Against back wall pushes it flat to the back wall. The plan draws each couch with its back edge thick, and the 3D view shows a frame, seat cushion, arms and back in the fabric's color.

Risers. Add a riser (a platform the seats stand on) with its width, depth and height. A couch whose middle is over a riser sits on it by itself: it rises with the riser in 3D, a new couch lands on the first riser, and a new riser is sized to fit under the first couch. Move or resize a couch on a riser and it stays inside the riser's edges; move the riser and the couches on it come along. Sit on a riser snaps a couch onto the nearest riser, and Fit to couch resizes and turns a riser to fit the couch on it. A line under each item says what it sits on, the ear height for a seat there, or that the couch hangs off the edge. The 3D view draws the riser with a step in front.

The Speaker placement page has the same 3D switch: pale cabinets are the recommended spots, dark ones are yours, and a colored line (green close, red off) joins each recommended spot to where yours is. Height speakers hang from a soffit's underside where a soffit is overhead.

  • Ring time estimate: until you have measured the room, the treatment plan shows a rough ring time that counts your couches (leather and vinyl soak up the least, velvet and thick fabric the most). A real system report replaces it.
  • Back at your ears: if a seat sits on a couch whose back is higher than about 3 ft and within 3 ft of your head, the plan says the couch back reflects sound at your ears.
  • Back wall: a tall couch filling most of the back wall lowers how high the back-wall treatment ranks (it is never removed). A couch does not absorb bass, so bass traps there still help.

Risers: press Add riser if your seats stand on a platform. Give its width across the room, its depth, its height, and where its middle is; Against back wall pushes it flat to the back wall. The plan draws it dashed with its height, and the 3D view shows a box with any couch on it lifted. Ear height (Room, Listening positions) is measured from the floor, so for a seat on a riser it should be the riser plus sitting height: the treatment plan reminds you when it looks like the riser isn't counted.

The numbers are typical published values and are rough. Turning the room (Turn left, Turn right, Mirror) turns the couches and risers with it. The assistant can record the screen, furniture and soffits for you when you describe them in chat, and the phone room builder can propose them from photos (you check them before saving).

Every-channel report and receiver setup sheet

After the All speakers check, the page shows a Receiver setup sheet: for each speaker, what to change in the receiver's own setup menu (the level trim in dB and the distance in feet), always relative to the front left. Changes under 1 dB or 0.3 ms are left alone, trims are rounded to 0.5 dB and distances to 0.1 ft, and a speaker that is far too early or loud is marked check wiring instead of getting a number (that points at a wrong terminal or assignment, not a setting). If you copied your receiver's current levels and distances onto the Speakers page before the check, the sheet also shows now → set to. If you change those values afterwards it warns you to run the check again. With Dirac on, the sheet is for verifying, not changing.

Channel by channel (open it under the sheet) adds how well each speaker's tone matches the front left (200 Hz to 8 kHz: under 2 dB good, 2 to 4 watch, over 4 fix) and how low it plays (the frequencies where it is 3 and 6 dB down; information only). Heights and wides can't be reached with the 7.1 test file, so they are listed as not measured. Copy puts the sheet on the clipboard and Print sheet opens a printable page; the calibration report has a Channel by channel section too.

Backups

Tools → Backups keeps your work safe against a bad SD card or a failed update. A backup is one zip file with your room, couches and soffits, saved tunes and slots, measurements, mic calibration, results and the fix log, journal, hardware database, chat and settings. It never holds your API keys, PIN or tokens, and htTamer refuses to keep a backup that contains one, so the file is safe to copy anywhere.

  • When: automatically every week, right before an update installs, and when you press Back up now. The newest five are kept (the last one made before an update is always kept).
  • Restore: pick a backup and press Restore. htTamer first makes a safety copy of what you have now, then puts the backup back and reloads the page. Your keys, PIN and this computer's own settings (audio device, miniDSP path) are not changed. A file that isn't an htTamer backup, or comes from a newer htTamer, is refused.
  • Download and upload: download a backup to keep it elsewhere, or add one from a file (up to 50 MB) and then restore it from the list.
  • The assistant can list and make backups; restoring one always asks for your OK first.
  • Dropbox: under Keep a copy in Dropbox, make a free Dropbox app once (dropbox.com/developers/apps → Create app → Scoped access → App folder; on its Permissions tab tick files.content.write and files.content.read and Submit), paste its App key, press Connect, allow access on the Dropbox page and paste the code it shows. htTamer never sees your Dropbox password and keeps only a sign-in token on this device (never in a backup). After that every new backup is also sent to the htTamer-backups folder in your app's Dropbox folder (the newest five stay, plus the one made before an update). Show what is in Dropbox lists them and Bring back adds one to the list here, where Restore works as usual; Disconnect forgets the sign-in. Backups are not encrypted: anyone with access to your Dropbox can read what is in them (your room, tunes and measurements, but no keys or PIN).
  • A second copy: under Keep a second copy off this device type the folder of a mounted USB stick or network share and every new backup is copied there as well.

Room treatment

Setup → Room treatment plans acoustic treatment (bass traps, absorption panels, a ceiling cloud, the back wall, a rug) for your room. It works offline from what htTamer already knows: the room size, seats and speaker and sub positions, the room modes, and your latest system report (ring time, early reflections, left/right differences) and check-up (bass ringing per band). Nothing plays.

  • What the measurements say: the problems treatment could help with, ranked by how much it would really help. "Predicted" means it comes from the room's size alone; run a system report and a check-up for the real thing.
  • The plan: each item says what to use (size, thickness, air gap), how many, roughly what it costs, where it goes (in feet from the walls) and what you'll hear. Items are grouped as Do this first, Next and Nice to have. The drawing shows the spots from above; the dots on the side walls are the first-reflection points, where the front speakers' sound bounces off the wall to each seat (worked out from your speaker and seat positions and the room's real shape; record where your speakers really are on the Speakers page for exact spots).
  • Be realistic: EQ fixes peaks; treatment fixes ringing, reflections and decay. A porous panel only absorbs well down to about 1000 ÷ its depth in inches Hz (a 4″ panel with a 2″ gap ≈ 200 Hz), so thin panels do nothing for bass. Below about 40 Hz the subs and EQ do almost all the work.
  • Cost and value: rough prices for what's still to buy, as ready-made panels or DIY (rockwool or fibreglass board, a wood frame and fabric, usually a third to half the price). Each item gets a verdict (Great value, Worth it, Worth it if the budget allows, Expensive for what it does, Little to gain) from how much it would help your room per dollar. The table lists the items in the order that gets you the most for your money, with the running total and the share of the plan's benefit, and a green line at the sweet spot, where about 70% of the improvement is done. The two side walls always count as one purchase, since panels on one side only pull the image sideways. With a budget set, htTamer shows what it buys and marks the rest. Prices are rough US ranges and the benefit is an estimate for ordering, not a promise; "predicted" items are worth confirming with a system report and a check-up before you spend.
  • Your constraints: walls you can't treat (the plan offers freestanding panels instead), a budget (items beyond it are marked), how visible it may be, and furniture. The assistant can save these for you and adjust the plan ("Ask the assistant").
  • What you have installed: press Mark installed on an item, or add what you already have. Each change is logged in the journal and keeps a snapshot of your latest measurements; after a new system report and check-up, Before and after shows what changed (ring time, the strongest reflection, left/right spread, bass ringing).

When your front speakers are in the wall or ceiling (record their kind on the Speakers page), htTamer skips panels behind them and suggests wall-cavity fill, sealing and front-corner bass traps instead. It replaces the front wall treatment with a "Wall cavity behind your in-wall speakers" item that explains what to do.

How tests play through the receiver

  • Pi HDMI (best): the Pi plays the test file out of its HDMI port. htTamer switches the receiver to the input you picked, and back afterwards. Up to 7.1 channels.
  • Network: the receiver's network player fetches the file from htTamer. Stereo only.
  • USB stick: you press play yourself. The file is offered for download on the page.

Set the receiver volume for me plays soft pink noise first and raises the volume in small steps until the mic reads your test level (75 dB by default). It never goes above your maximum test volume (Settings), or above your current volume + 12 when none is set, and never above 70. If raising doesn't make it louder, it stops. The original volume, input, listening mode, sub level and processing (Late Night, Cinema Filter, tone) are put back afterwards, including when you press Stop.

Receivers and the hardware database

Supported receivers (Survey → Receiver → Make, or Auto-detect from the address): Onkyo, Integra and Pioneer (eISCP; tested on a TX-RZ50), Denon and Marantz (telnet; follows Denon's published protocol), and JBL Synthesis SDP-55/58 and SDR-35/38 (Harman's IP protocol on port 50000; turn Control on in the unit's network settings). The last two aren't tested on hardware yet. A JBL Synthesis unit also reports its whole speaker setup (sizes, crossovers, distances and levels), which Copy to Speakers on the Receiver page fills in for you. Turn network control on in the receiver's menu. The Pi-HDMI input list follows the make.

Hardware database (Tools → AI analysis): htTamer's built-in knowledge covers specific models. For anything else, models can be recorded in the hardware database, and with Let the assistant build the hardware database ticked the assistant does it for you whenever a model comes up, looking up the maker's specs on the web and saving the source. htTamer uses what's recorded: a receiver's control protocol picks its driver, a ported sub's safe limit stops EQ boosts below it, and speaker specs inform the advice. Every entry is listed there and in the journal, and can be removed.

Turning on the AI

The AI features (the chat assistant, the room analysis, AI room treatment plans and the phone room builder) are optional and need an AI provider. Everything else works without one. Pick one of two ways in Tools → AI analysis (or the Turn on the AI assistant step of the first-start setup).

With an API key (bring your own key). You pay the provider per use; htTamer has a daily request cap (300 by default) so it can't run up a bill.

  1. Make a key with the provider you want: Anthropic (best tested) at console.anthropic.com → API keys, OpenAI at platform.openai.com → API keys, or Google Gemini at aistudio.google.com → Get API key. The provider may ask you to add credit first.
  2. In htTamer, choose that AI provider, paste the key into API key and press Save.
  3. Press Load list and pick a Model (or keep the suggested one), then press Test. A ✓ with a short reply from the AI means you're done; otherwise it shows what went wrong.

The key is kept only on the device running htTamer (in its config folder), is only sent to that provider, and is never shown again, put in a backup, or in a settings file unless you tick it. Remove key deletes it.

With a local model (free, runs on your own computer). Any OpenAI-compatible server works; the two easiest are Ollama and LM Studio, on a computer with a decent graphics card. On a Pi, run the model on another computer on your network.

  1. Install Ollama (ollama.com) and download a model, e.g. ollama pull qwen3; or install LM Studio (lmstudio.ai), download a model and start its server (Developer tab).
  2. If htTamer runs on a different computer (such as the Pi), let the server answer the network: for Ollama set the environment variable OLLAMA_HOST=0.0.0.0 and restart it; in LM Studio turn on Serve on Local Network. That computer's firewall may ask once: allow it on private networks.
  3. In htTamer choose Local model (Ollama, LM Studio…) and type the Local server address: http://<that computer's address>:11434/v1 for Ollama or …:1234/v1 for LM Studio (http://127.0.0.1:11434/v1 if it's the same computer). No key is needed.
  4. Press Save, Load list, pick your model, and press Test.

Local models are slower and less sure-footed than the big providers, especially with the assistant's tools; pick the largest model your graphics card holds. To read photos (the phone room builder), the model has to understand images; if yours doesn't, set a Photo model that does. The safety rules are the same either way: anything that plays sound or changes something still asks you first.

The assistant

The chat knows your system: the playing tune, measurements, reports, the fix log, your equipment and the live EQ. Ask it what a result means or what to do next, or ask it to run a test. Anything that plays sound or changes a tune asks for your approval first, with a one-line description. You can tell it about your speakers, subs and room and it records them (each change is logged). It can read the room treatment plan, explain it and adjust it to your budget, looks and the walls you can't treat, and record what you've put up. It needs an AI provider: an API key or a local model (see Turning on the AI). Without one, a built-in helper answers common questions.

What keeps it safe. The assistant only acts through htTamer's own tools, and htTamer checks every one itself, whatever the AI says:

  • Your approval for anything that plays sound or changes the miniDSP, the receiver or a tune. An approval you don't answer within 15 minutes expires, and after 4 approval requests for one message it has to stop and talk to you.
  • Hard limits: never slot 1; the receiver volume can't be raised; receiver sub and center level at most +6 dB, tone at most +6; live EQ boosts at most +3 dB and none where a ported sub could be damaged; test tones only on a sub output, 20-200 Hz.
  • Rest breaks: at most 8 sound tests in 10 minutes, from the chat or a connected AI app.
  • Your hardware records stay yours: the assistant can add specs, but only you can change a receiver's control protocol or lower a sub's protection limit.
  • It ignores instructions in data: web pages, notes and results are treated as information, never as orders; keys and tokens are removed before anything reaches the AI.
  • Stop: the Stop button in the chat ends the current request at once (no more AI requests or actions).
  • Read and advise only (Tools → AI analysis → Assistant safety): it can look and explain but not act. Also there: a daily cap on AI requests (300 by default) so a stuck conversation can't run up your bill.
  • Every action it takes, and everything it was refused, is in the activity log.

Using htTamer from another AI app (MCP)

Tools → MCP lets an AI app you already use, such as Claude Desktop or Claude Code, work with htTamer through MCP (the Model Context Protocol). It gets the same tools as htTamer's own assistant: it can read the state, results, journal, plan and reports, and, if you allow it, run tests and change things. It runs on your AI app's account, not on htTamer's API key, and htTamer has to be running while you use it.

  • Let AI clients connect turns it on. It's off until you turn it on; while off, htTamer answers no MCP requests and its MCP command won't start.
  • Every connection needs a credential, even from the computer htTamer runs on. Make a token creates an access token: it's shown once, so copy it then (htTamer keeps only a fingerprint of it). Make a new token replaces it and Revoke the token cuts off every app using it. The MCP command for Claude Desktop and Claude Code, run as you on the computer htTamer runs on, uses a private key htTamer keeps in its config folder instead, so it needs no token; HTTP clients and a Pi reached over ssh need the token.
  • Actions decides what happens with tools that play sound or change the miniDSP or receiver (tests, applying a tune, switching slots, receiver controls, fixes). Off: the AI app can only read. Ask me each time (recommended): before each one, htTamer asks you in the AI app with the same one-line description the assistant uses; apps that can't ask (they lack MCP "elicitation") are refused. My AI client asks me: htTamer relies on the AI app's own permission prompt, so don't set that app to allow htTamer's tools automatically.
  • The rules don't change: slot 1 (your original) is never written, the receiver volume can only be lowered (tests set it with their guarded auto-level and put it back), and API keys, tokens and the PIN are never sent to the AI app.

Connecting. The page shows the exact text to copy. For Claude Desktop, add it to claude_desktop_config.json (Settings → Developer → Edit Config) and restart it. For Claude Code, run the claude mcp add line it shows. The app starts htTamer's mcp command, which passes each request to the running dashboard. On a Pi the AI app on your computer starts that command over ssh: set up ssh sign-in with a key first, then save the token on the Pi with the command the page shows (it goes in ~/.httamer-mcp-token, readable only by you). Other MCP apps can use the HTTP address /mcp with the header Authorization: Bearer and your token.

Dirac Live

What makes Dirac out of date: anything that changes the subs' levels or EQ after Dirac measured: a different sub tune, Live EQ bands playing, or Live EQ saved into a tune. Timing-only changes (a blend fix's delay, polarity or phase filter) keep Dirac's correction valid. htTamer asks before a change that would make Dirac out of date, and Home says when it is.

Order of work: sub tune first, then Dirac Live with htTamer's target curve (the Dirac step gives you the file), then the blend test with Dirac on, then verify and a system report. While Dirac is measuring, close htTamer's level meter and don't start an htTamer test: both use the same mic. A new sub tune means Dirac should be run again.

Night mode

Press Night at the top of any page (or Tools → Automation) to cut all bass from the subs. htTamer mutes the miniDSP's master output, so whatever the receiver plays, the subs stay silent; nothing is written to any slot. It stays on until you turn it off, or until the time you set under Switch off by itself at (say 07:30). Switching slots, by hand or automatically, keeps the subs muted, and if something unmutes them (the miniDSP remote, say) htTamer mutes them again within a minute. Sound tests wait while it's on. Note that with the subs muted, the bass your receiver sends to them is gone too: speakers set to "small" won't play it instead.

Automatic slot and watchdog

Automatic slot (Tools → Automation): choose a slot for any receiver input or listening mode, for example slot 3 for the streaming box and slot 2 for the CD player, or a slot just for Pure Audio. When the receiver's input or mode changes, htTamer switches the miniDSP to that slot within a few seconds. A listening-mode rule wins over an input rule. It acts only when something changes, so a slot you pick by hand stays until the next change, and it pauses while tests run (tests switch the receiver's input themselves).

Watchdog: every minute, and right after the receiver's input changes, htTamer checks that the receiver's sub level is still the one to keep. An Onkyo resets a sub level set over the network whenever you change inputs, which quietly undoes a blend fix. With Put the sub level back by itself it sets it again; otherwise it just tells you. It also tells you if the miniDSP isn't on htTamer's slot or isn't listening to the receiver. The level to keep is the last one htTamer set, unless you type your own. Everything it puts back appears as a notice and in the activity log. Nothing plays.

Receiver auto-off

Tools → Automation → Receiver auto-off puts the receiver in standby after a set time (30 minutes by default) with nothing playing. Playing means any signal on the miniDSP's inputs, anyone changing the receiver's volume, input or mode, or anything htTamer does (tests, tuning, the assistant, a button in the app); each of those starts the time again. A paused film counts as nothing playing; a quiet scene doesn't, so it won't cut off a movie. A notice appears 5 minutes before it switches off. If htTamer can't read the miniDSP it never switches the receiver off, and after the Pi restarts the time starts fresh. Your receiver may also have its own Auto Standby in its setup menu; that one can't see what htTamer is doing.

Blind A/B test

Verify → Blind A/B tells you which of two slots you really prefer, without knowing which is which. Pick the two slots and the number of rounds, then play your own music or a film scene at your usual volume. In each round the slots hide behind buttons 1 and 2 in a new random order: switch between them as often as you like and pick the one you prefer. At the end htTamer reveals which slot won how often and how likely that split is by pure chance (under 5% is a real preference), and puts the slot you were on back. Louder usually sounds better, so compare tunes at the same level; 8 or more rounds give a clearer answer. Comparing with slot 1 (your original setup) is a good way to check htTamer's tune is an improvement.

Home Assistant

Tools → Automation → Home Assistant connects htTamer to Home Assistant with its built-in RESTful integration, with no add-on to install. Press Make a token, add it to Home Assistant's secrets.yaml as shown, and paste the configuration under it into configuration.yaml. You get sensors for the playing slot, its tune, the receiver's sub level, night mode and the automatic slot, and actions to switch the slot, night mode and the automatic slot, ready for a "movie night" script or an automation. Every request needs the token, which only works for these few silent actions (it can't play tests or change tunes). Revoke it there at any time.

Updates

Tools → Updates keeps the Windows app and the Pi up to date from htTamer's public GitHub releases, with nothing to set up. htTamer checks once a day and pops up a note when a new version is out; Install does the rest. On Windows the app closes, updates and opens again; on the Pi the update is checked before it's used, and if the new version doesn't start the previous one comes back by itself. Settings, tunes and measurements are always kept. (Following a fork instead? Set its repository there; a private one also needs a read-only token.) A minute or two after an update (or the first start), htTamer runs its silent hardware check by itself, once per version, and shows under Updates whether the miniDSP, the receiver and the mic all answer, so a problem shows up straight away rather than at your next tuning session.

Network name

Tools → Network name sets the address phones and computers use: http://<name>.local:<port>. The name is letters, digits and dashes; the port is 8765 unless something else on that computer already uses it (any from 1024 to 65535). On the Pi a new name renames the Pi itself and a new port restarts htTamer on it; the dashboard moves a few seconds later, and the Pi's IP address keeps working (with the new port). On Windows htTamer normally listens only to that computer. Tick Open htTamer to my home network (here, or on the installer's Network page, which also asks for the name and port) and start htTamer again (a new port also applies then): phones on your network then open it at the name you chose, which htTamer announces itself, so the computer's own name is left alone. The first time, Windows may ask whether htTamer may use the network: allow it on private networks. Like the Pi, network mode asks for a PIN, which you choose on the Windows computer itself before anyone else can sign in.

Bugs and feedback

Tools → Feedback turns a bug report or an idea into a new issue on htTamer's GitHub page. Pick the kind, write what happened (or your idea) and press Open on GitHub: the issue opens already filled in, you check it, and you press Submit new issue there (a free GitHub account is needed). htTamer never sends anything by itself. Include diagnostics adds htTamer's version, Windows or Pi, the receiver model, the miniDSP slot and the latest errors from the activity log, with IP addresses, keys, e-mail addresses and names removed; open See the diagnostics to read exactly what's added, or copy them if they're too long for the link.

Built-in safeguards

  • Nothing plays unless you start it (or approve it in the chat).
  • Slot 1 (your original setup) is never written by tuning, tests or the assistant; only you can overwrite it, after confirming.
  • The receiver volume is only raised by the guarded auto-level and is always put back.
  • No EQ boost where a sub's port mode can be damaged, and the forbidden knob combinations are flagged on Home.
  • Tests stop on overload (mic clipping or far above the test level).

When something goes wrong

The miniDSP listens to the receiver normally. htTamer switches it to USB only while it measures, and switches it back by itself as soon as nothing needs USB; if you pick USB yourself at the top of the page, it goes back to the receiver after 10 minutes.

  • Start with Setup → Check the hardware (silent). It reads the miniDSP (slot, input, mute) and the receiver, listens to the mic for a second, and checks the Pi's HDMI, without playing anything or changing anything. On the Pi, subtune smoke does the same from a terminal.
  • A test hears nothing: is the receiver on, unmuted and on the right input? For Pi HDMI, the receiver should show a PCM signal while the test plays.
  • "Another step is running": wait, or press Stop in the header.
  • Live EQ won't send: the miniDSP must be playing htTamer's slot (Tunes & slots), and nothing else may be running.
  • The sub level changed by itself: on Onkyo, a sub level set over the network is temporary and resets when the input changes. htTamer sets it again after its own tests.
  • The result doesn't match what was expected: the mic sat differently, a sub knob moved, or a door was open. Verify again; re-measure if it persists.
  • Everything htTamer does is in System → Activity.

Words used in the app

MLP
Main listening position: the seat that matters most.
Slot
One of the miniDSP's four presets. htTamer uses slot 3; slot 1 is your original.
Goal / target curve
The response htTamer aims for: flat, plus a bass lift (house curve) below a corner frequency.
PEQ band
One parametric EQ filter: frequency, gain and Q.
Q
How narrow a band is. Higher means narrower.
Room mode
A resonance set by the room's dimensions: a peak at some seats and a dip at others.
Crossover
Where the receiver hands bass from the speakers to the subs (often 80 Hz).
Blend / dB lost
How well subs and speakers add up around the crossover. Lost dB is bass canceled there.
Polarity
Whether a driver pushes or pulls on the same signal. Flipping a sub's polarity can fix a cancellation.
All-pass filter
A filter that shifts phase around one frequency without changing level, used to line up subs and speakers.
First-reflection point
The spot on a wall, the ceiling or the floor where a speaker's sound bounces straight to your ears. Treating it sharpens the image and dialogue.
Bass trap
Thick absorption (or a tuned membrane) in corners, where bass pressure is highest. It shortens how long bass rings.
RT60 / ring time
How long sound takes to die away by 60 dB. 0.2-0.4 s suits a home theater.
Headroom
How much louder something can go before it distorts or clips.
